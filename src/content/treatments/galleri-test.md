## A blood test that looks for a shared cancer signal

Most cancer screening looks for one cancer at a time, and only a handful of cancers have an established screening test at all. The Galleri test, developed by [GRAIL](https://www.galleri.com/), takes a different approach: it examines DNA fragments circulating in the blood and looks for methylation patterns associated with cancer, across more than 50 types.

It is a single blood draw, prescribed and reviewed by a physician. When a cancer signal is detected, the test also predicts the likely origin in the body, which tells the physician where to look first.

## What it is not

This is the part that deserves your attention before anything else.

**It is not FDA-approved.** The Galleri test has not been cleared or approved by the Food and Drug Administration. It is a laboratory developed test, available by prescription only.

**It does not replace routine screening.** Galleri is intended to be used in addition to the cancer screening your provider already recommends — mammography, colonoscopy, cervical screening and the rest. It does not substitute for any of them.

**It does not detect all cancers.** Some cancers shed very little detectable material into the blood, particularly at an early stage. A test that finds a signal for many cancers is not a test that finds every cancer.

**A negative result does not rule out cancer.** No Cancer Signal Detected means no recognised signal was found. It is not a clean bill of health, and it is not a reason to skip anything else.

**A positive result is not a diagnosis.** Cancer Signal Detected requires confirmatory evaluation through established diagnostic procedures, such as imaging. GRAIL reports that around 62% of detected signals are confirmed as cancer — which also means a significant number are not, and that the interval before a diagnosis is confirmed or excluded can be an anxious one.

## Who it is intended for

Galleri is recommended for adults at elevated risk of cancer, such as those aged 50 or older. Beyond age, the question is a clinical one: your personal and family history, your other risk factors, and — a question worth sitting with — what you would actually do with each possible result.

Some people find the information valuable. Others find the uncertainty of a false positive, or the false reassurance of a negative, harder to live with than not knowing. Both responses are legitimate, and the physician will talk it through with you rather than push you toward the test.

## How it works here

The test is ordered by a physician after a consultation, drawn as a single blood sample, and processed by GRAIL's laboratory. The result returns to the ordering physician, who reviews it with you, explains what it means in your specific situation, and arranges appropriate follow-up if a signal is found.
