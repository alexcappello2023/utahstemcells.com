## Living with rheumatoid arthritis

Rheumatoid arthritis asks a lot of you. The morning stiffness that takes hours to ease, the joints that flare without warning, the fatigue that no one else quite sees — it's a daily balancing act, often across several joints at once. If you're managing RA, you already know how much work it takes, and how much you'd give for a little more comfort in the joints that hurt most.

Rheumatoid arthritis is an [autoimmune](/autoimmune-conditions/) disease in which the immune system attacks the joints, causing inflammation, pain and damage over time. It is a chronic condition, and it is typically co-managed with a rheumatologist who directs the disease-modifying care that keeps it in check.

## Where a regenerative approach may — and may not — fit

Let's be clear and honest up front: **regenerative medicine is not a treatment or a cure for rheumatoid arthritis.** RA is an autoimmune disease that requires ongoing medical management, and nothing we offer replaces the care your rheumatologist provides.

What some patients explore is different and more limited. Alongside their existing rheumatology care, they consider regenerative options such as [stem cell joint regeneration](/stem-cell-joint-regeneration/) or [IV stem cell therapy](/iv-stem-cell-therapy/) to help **manage pain in a specific joint** — not to alter the disease itself. Whether that's reasonable in your case depends on your disease activity, your current treatment and your overall health, and it's considered only after a physician evaluation and in the context of your complete plan.
