You've rested it. You've iced it. Maybe you've done physical therapy, taken anti-inflammatories, even had a cortisone shot. And yet the ache in your shoulder is still there — catching when you reach overhead, waking you at night, quietly shrinking what you're willing to do. When [shoulder pain](/shoulder-pain/) drags on for months, it's fair to ask whether there's an option beyond "wait and see" or "eventually, surgery."

For some people, **regenerative medicine** is that middle path worth understanding.

## Why shoulder pain lingers

The shoulder is the most mobile joint in the body, which also makes it one of the easiest to irritate. Persistent pain often traces back to:

- **Rotator cuff wear or tears** — the tendons and muscles that stabilize the joint
- **Tendinitis and bursitis** — inflammation from overuse or repetitive motion
- **Osteoarthritis** — gradual cartilage wear, the same process behind [osteoarthritis](/osteoarthritis/) elsewhere
- **Old injuries** that never fully settled

The common thread is that these are soft-tissue and joint problems that don't always heal well on their own — which is why pain can linger long after you expected it to fade.

## When the usual options aren't enough

Rest, anti-inflammatories, physical therapy and cortisone injections all have their place. But they mostly work by **calming symptoms** — they don't set out to support the underlying repair. That's why relief can be temporary, and why so many people cycle through the same treatments without lasting progress.

At that point the conversation often jumps straight to surgery. For some shoulders that's the right answer. For many, though, it's worth exploring a less invasive step first.

## How regenerative medicine approaches shoulder pain

Regenerative medicine takes a different angle: instead of only masking pain, the goal is to **support your body's natural repair processes** in the irritated tendon or joint. ## PRP, stem cells, or both?

These are two different regenerative tools, and it's worth knowing which is which:

- **PRP (platelet-rich plasma)** is drawn from a small sample of **your own blood**, concentrated for its growth factors, and injected into the shoulder.
- **Stem cell therapy** adds regenerative signaling from **ethically donated umbilical cord and amniotic tissue** — prepared in a lab, not harvested from your body. We do not use autologous (your-own) stem cells such as fat- or bone-marrow-derived cells.

Depending on your shoulder, the physician may recommend one or a combination. We break the comparison down fully in [PRP vs Stem Cells: Which Treatment Is Right for Your Joint Pain?](/prp-vs-stem-cells-which-treatment-is-right-for-your-joint-pain/) — a helpful read if you're weighing your options.

## Who might it help — and who might not?

Regenerative approaches tend to be most worth considering for **mild to moderate** shoulder problems: nagging tendinitis, early rotator cuff wear, or ongoing [joint pain](/joint-pain/) that hasn't responded to conservative care. Good candidates are generally in reasonable health and have realistic expectations — the goal is to reduce pain and improve function, not to guarantee a brand-new shoulder.

It's less likely to be the answer for a large, full-thickness rotator cuff tear or a severely degenerated joint, where a surgical repair may be more appropriate. The honest way to find out which group you're in is a proper evaluation — and a provider willing to tell you when surgery would actually serve you better. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can regenerative medicine fix a rotator cuff tear?
It may help with mild wear or partial irritation by supporting natural repair, but it is not a substitute for surgery in the case of a large, full-thickness tear. A physician evaluation — including imaging — is the way to know which applies to you.

### Are the stem cells taken from my own body?
No. At Utah Stem Cells the stem cells come from ethically donated umbilical cord and amniotic tissue, prepared in a laboratory. Only PRP is drawn from you, from a small blood sample. We do not use fat- or bone-marrow-derived (autologous) stem cells.

### Is it painful, and is there downtime?
Treatment is minimally invasive and performed in-office. Most patients tolerate it well with little to no downtime. Your provider explains exactly what to expect and how to care for the shoulder afterward.

### How soon might I feel a difference?
