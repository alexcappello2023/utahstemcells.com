Choosing a stem cell clinic is a medical decision, and the field attracts clinics of very different quality. Here are five things worth checking before you commit anywhere. For a longer list of warning signs, see our guide to [red flags when choosing a stem cell clinic](/how-to-choose-a-stem-cell-clinic-red-flags-to-avoid/).

## 1. A physician-led medical clinic

Ask who actually performs your procedure. A dedicated medical clinic run by physicians is a different thing from a business that hires someone to do injections on its behalf. You are entitled to know who will be treating you.

## 2. Training you can verify

Ask where the physician trained, which board certifications they hold, and how long they have been performing these procedures. Do not rely on a website alone: you can confirm a license with the state licensing board.

## 3. Honesty about FDA status

Stem cell therapies for joint pain and similar uses are generally not FDA-approved. A clinic that says so plainly is being straight with you. Be wary of anyone who promises a cure, a guaranteed result, or says there is no risk.

## 4. Transparency about the cells

Ask what type of cells are used, where they come from, how they are prepared, and what testing supports their quality. A good clinic answers plainly. At Utah Stem Cells the stem cells come from ethically donated umbilical cord and amniotic tissue and are prepared in a laboratory; they are not taken from your own body. The PRP is drawn from your own blood.

## 5. An evaluation first, and a willingness to say no

A trustworthy clinic examines you and reviews your history before recommending anything, and tells you when you are not a good candidate. If you are being offered treatment before anyone has assessed you, that is worth questioning.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I check a doctor's credentials?

Ask for their training and board certifications, then verify their license with your state's licensing board.

### Is stem cell therapy FDA-approved?

No. Stem cell therapies like these are not FDA-approved for treating specific conditions, and results vary from person to person.
