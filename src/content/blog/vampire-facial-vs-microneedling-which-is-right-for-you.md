If you have been researching ways to refresh your skin's texture, tone, or fine lines, you have probably run into two treatments that sound similar but are not identical: microneedling and the vampire facial. Both use tiny needles to create controlled micro-injuries that encourage your skin to rebuild collagen, but they are not interchangeable, and choosing the wrong one for your skin's needs can mean underwhelming results or unnecessary downtime. Understanding what actually separates them can help you have a more informed conversation with your provider about which approach fits your goals.

## What is microneedling?

Standard microneedling uses a device fitted with a cluster of fine, sterile needles to create thousands of microscopic punctures in the skin's surface. This controlled "injury" triggers your body's natural wound-healing response, which includes new collagen and elastin production. Over the following weeks, skin can appear smoother, firmer, and more even in tone. Many providers pair microneedling with a topical serum applied during the session to support hydration and calm the skin as it heals. [Microneedling](/microneedling/) is often used on its own or combined with radiofrequency energy, as in [Morpheus8](/morpheus8/), for deeper skin tightening.

## What is a vampire facial?

A vampire facial starts the same way, with a microneedling device creating those same micro-channels, but adds one important ingredient: platelet-rich plasma (PRP) drawn from your own blood. A small blood sample is spun in a centrifuge to concentrate the platelets and growth factors, which are then applied to the skin during and after the needling process. Because PRP is autologous, meaning it comes from your own body rather than a donor or synthetic source, it is designed to work with your skin's own healing signals rather than introducing anything foreign. This is a distinct category of regenerative skincare from our [vampire facial](/vampire-facial/) and [vampire facelift](/vampire-facelift/) services, which use this same PRP-driven approach.

It is worth noting that this PRP process is different from stem cell therapy. At Utah Stem Cells, the regenerative cells we use for joint and wellness treatments come from donated umbilical cord and amniotic tissue processed in a lab, not from a patient's own fat or bone marrow. PRP for facial rejuvenation, by contrast, genuinely is autologous, drawn from your own blood on the day of treatment. Your provider can walk you through which biological approach is being used for any given treatment so you know exactly what you are receiving.

## How the two treatments compare

The needling mechanics are similar, but the added PRP step in a vampire facial is thought to influence how the skin responds:

- **Healing signal**: Microneedling alone relies on the injury response itself. A vampire facial adds concentrated growth factors from PRP, which some patients and providers believe may support a more robust healing and collagen-building process, though individual response varies and results are not guaranteed.
- **Best-suited concerns**: Standard microneedling is commonly chosen for general texture, fine lines, and mild tone irregularities. Vampire facials are more often discussed for more visible acne scarring, sun damage, or patients wanting a more comprehensive rejuvenation approach.
- **Downtime**: Both are minimally invasive with modest recovery. Expect redness and some sensitivity for one to a few days after either treatment, with microneedling alone sometimes resolving slightly faster since there is no additional PRP application step.
- **Session count**: Both typically require a series of sessions spaced several weeks apart, since collagen remodeling happens gradually over time, not in a single visit.

Neither treatment is inherently "better" in every case. The right choice depends on your skin's specific needs, your tolerance for downtime, and what your provider observes during an in-person evaluation.

## Who tends to be a good candidate

Good candidates for either treatment are generally in good overall health, do not have active acne breakouts, skin infections, or certain bleeding or clotting disorders, and have realistic expectations about gradual, cumulative improvement rather than an overnight transformation. Patients considering PRP specifically should mention any blood-related conditions or medications, such as blood thinners, during their consultation. Pregnant or breastfeeding patients are typically advised to wait. Because skin type, medical history, and treatment goals vary so much from person to person, candidacy is always determined individually by a qualified provider, not by a generic checklist.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a vampire facial hurt more than microneedling?

Both treatments use numbing cream to minimize discomfort, and most patients describe the sensation as mild pressure or light scratching rather than significant pain. The addition of PRP does not typically change the discomfort level of the needling itself.

### How many sessions will I need?

This varies by individual, but many providers recommend a series of three or more sessions spaced roughly four to six weeks apart for either treatment, since collagen remodeling is a gradual process. Your provider will build a plan based on your specific skin and goals.

### Is a vampire facial the same as stem cell therapy?

No. A vampire facial uses platelet-rich plasma drawn from your own blood. It does not involve stem cells. The regenerative cell therapies Utah Stem Cells offers for other concerns use donated umbilical cord and amniotic tissue processed in a lab, which is a separate category of treatment entirely.

### Can I combine microneedling or a vampire facial with other treatments?

Many patients combine these treatments with options like Morpheus8 or dermal fillers as part of a broader skincare plan. Whether combination treatment makes sense for you depends on your skin's condition and goals, which your provider can evaluate during a consultation.

### Are results permanent?

No aesthetic treatment produces permanent results, and skin continues to age naturally afterward. Most patients maintain their results with periodic follow-up sessions and a consistent skincare routine at home.

### Who should avoid these treatments?

Patients with active acne breakouts, certain skin infections, bleeding or clotting disorders, or who are pregnant or breastfeeding are generally advised to avoid or postpone these treatments. A provider evaluation is the only reliable way to confirm candidacy for your specific situation.
