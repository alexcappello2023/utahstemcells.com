Most cancer screening looks for one cancer at a time — and only a handful of cancers have a standard screening test at all.

The Galleri test works differently. It is a single blood draw that looks for a signal shared by more than 50 types of cancer. When a signal is found, the test also predicts where in the body it is likely to have come from, which tells the physician where to look first.

Galleri is now available at Utah Stem Cells. It is a prescription test, ordered and reviewed by a physician, and it is used in addition to the screening you already do — not in place of it.

If you are 50 or older, or have an elevated risk of cancer, it may be worth a conversation.

This is the first thing we say about the Galleri test, before anything else.

Galleri is intended to be used alongside the cancer screening your healthcare provider already recommends — mammography, colonoscopy, cervical screening and the rest. It does not replace any of them.

Those tests look for specific cancers in ways a blood test cannot replicate. Stopping them because you have had a Galleri test would leave you less protected, not more.

What Galleri adds is coverage across cancers that have no routine screening test at all.

Used together, the two do different jobs. Used instead of one another, something gets missed.

Breast. Colon. Cervical. Prostate. Lung. For these, established screening tests exist, and you probably know whether you are up to date on them.

For most other cancers, there is no routine screening at all. Pancreatic, ovarian, liver, esophageal and many more are usually found only once they begin to cause symptoms.

The Galleri test looks for a signal shared by more than 50 types of cancer from a single blood sample — including many that have no screening test of their own. When a signal is found, it predicts where in the body it is likely to have originated.

It does not detect all cancers, and it does not replace the screening you already do. But it looks where routine screening does not.

Before you consider the Galleri test, you should know what each result does and does not tell you.

No Cancer Signal Detected does not mean you are cancer-free. It means no recognized signal was found. The test does not detect all cancers, and false negatives occur. Your routine screening matters exactly as much afterward as it did before.

Cancer Signal Detected is not a diagnosis. It calls for confirmatory evaluation through established procedures such as imaging. GRAIL reports that around 62% of detected signals are confirmed as cancer — which also means a meaningful share are not, and that the wait for an answer can be an anxious one.

We would rather you knew all of this before the blood draw than after it.
